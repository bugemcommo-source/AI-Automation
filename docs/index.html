Stage by stage

What happens at each step

Three things are worth separating: what your customer experiences, what the machine is doing while they do it, and what gets permanently written down.

Stage 1

The post goes out

You write a caption once. It goes to nine platforms — each with its own tracking link hidden in it, and each trimmed to fit that platform's limits.

They see

A normal post in their feed with a link.

Behind it

Nine separate links minted, one per platform. X only allows 280 characters, so the caption is shortened there — but the link is never cut.

Written down

One row per platform: exact wording sent, length, whether it was shortened, when. social_post_log

Stage 2

Someone taps the link

The link doesn't go straight to your chat. It passes through, stamps a hidden code onto that person, and forwards them on — all in a fraction of a second.

They see

They tap, and a chat opens. That's it.

Behind it

A one-off code is attached so their later messages can be traced back to this exact post. Link-preview robots are spotted and excluded so they don't inflate your numbers.

Written down

Which post, which platform, when, and whether it was a real person. click_events

Stage 3

The chat answers

A greeting, then real answers to real questions — pricing, hours, location, how long a session runs. If it doesn't know, it says so instead of guessing.

They see

An instant, correct reply at any hour.

Behind it

The hidden code is swapped for the post that sent them. If they've messaged before, they keep their original post credit — a second visit can't steal it.

Written down

Both sides of every conversation, plus a contact record. messages contacts

Stage 4

They pick a slot

A short form: name, email, phone, preferred date and time. The system checks the calendar before it asks for a single peso.

They see

Either "your slot is held" with a payment link, or a polite "that one's gone — pick another", saying why.

Behind it

The slot is held for twenty minutes. If someone else's hold has expired, that slot quietly becomes available again.

Written down

The booking at "form submitted", plus the hold and its expiry. bookings slots

Stage 5

The deposit

They pay. Then — and this is the part worth paying attention to — the system checks the slot again before confirming anything.

They see

A confirmation naming the deposit paid and the balance still due on the day.

Behind it

Twenty minutes is long enough for a slot to be taken. If it's gone, the booking isn't confirmed and the deposit is returned rather than double-booking your studio.

Written down

The payment, the confirmed booking, the calendar entry. payments bookings

Stage 6

Receipt and congratulations

A receipt by email and a warm text message, sent the moment the money lands.

They see

An email receipt and an SMS. A calendar invite lands too.

Behind it

Both go out at once rather than one after the other, so a failure in one can't hold up the other.

Written down

Every message sent, to whom, and whether it arrived. notifications

Stage 7

The quiet ones get chased

Every hour, the system looks for people who started but stopped — and nudges them. Exactly once.

They see

One friendly reminder, by text if you have their number, otherwise by email. Never a second one.

Behind it

Anyone stuck for more than a day gets one nudge, and is then marked so nobody is ever pestered twice. People who only chatted and never reached the form are on this list too.

Written down

The nudge sent and a count against that person. notifications bookings

Stage 8

Everything lands in one place

A single page showing every post, click, conversation, booking, payment and follow-up — with buttons to send it wherever you want it.

You see

Headline numbers, which posts actually worked, and exactly who still needs chasing.

Behind it

All ten record books are read and combined in one place, so the numbers on the page and the numbers in an export can never disagree.

Written down

Nothing new — this stage only reads. Failures elsewhere land in ops_events

The careful bit

How two people can't book the same slot

This is the failure that costs you a customer and an apology. Here's how it's prevented — the bar in the middle is the slot itself, changing hands over the course of an afternoon.

Why this is worth mentioning

Most booking systems check availability once, at the start. The gap between "I'll take it" and "here's my card" is where double-bookings are born. This one checks twice — before the money and again after it — and treats a lost slot as a refund, not an apology.

When things go wrong

The parts nobody thinks to ask about

Most of the care in a system like this goes into the paths you hope never happen.

A payment fails

The booking simply isn't confirmed, the slot is released, and the customer is told plainly which of the three things went wrong.

Someone abandons a hold

Their twenty minutes lapse and the slot returns to the pool automatically. No manual tidying, no slot sitting dead.

A record fails to save

Logging never blocks the customer. A lost log line is annoying; a customer who never got their reply is worse.

Link-preview robots

When a chat app fetches your link to build a preview, that isn't counted as a person tapping it. Your click numbers stay honest.

Somebody messages twice

They keep the credit of the post that first brought them in. A later visit can't quietly steal it from the post that earned it.

Anything else

Any failure anywhere is caught, written down with the exact step that broke, and surfaced on the dashboard rather than disappearing.
